# P1.5.2 public-catalog replay

The first offline benchmark freezes 14 authored listening scenarios using 11 real MusicBrainz recording identities across five recording families. It covers studio and live recordings, a remix, compilation appearances, alternate credits, a cover, misleading identifiers, and ambiguous source claims. Five cases from two families train the learner. Nine cases from three other families are held out. The validator rejects overlap in observation groups or any candidate recording MBID, including alternatives.

## Review and evidence

- Reference labels were checked against specific MusicBrainz edition slots independently of the scorer and cross-checked against artist or label material where available. Artist material independently supports release context, while recording IDs come from MusicBrainz. [Nirvana Unplugged](https://shop.nirvana.com/products/mtv-unplugged), [Depeche Mode remix](https://archives.depechemode.com/discography/singles/41_enjoythesilence04.html), [Queen Greatest Hits](https://store.queenonline.com/products/platinum-collection-boxset), [Queen Wembley](https://store.queenonline.com/products/live-at-wembley-86-2cd), [David Bowie album](https://www.davidbowie.com/the-man-who-sold-the-world).
- The fixtures under `spec/fixtures/recording_resolution/real_corpus` retain the catalog projection, edition slot, source URL, retrieval time, response hash, and reference source. The scenario file stores labels separately. The frozen snapshot retains exactly the evidence and order generated by P1.5.1 before each simulated decision. Replaying fails if any snapshot drifts.
- This is a stratified convenience corpus of constructed scenarios. Candidate pools were assembled from known edition slots. Labels and replayed choices are benchmark data. There were no production observations, actual operator decisions, or independent listening panel. The corpus cannot measure candidate discovery recall or population accuracy. Misleading identifiers and correction sequences deliberately exercise failure modes.

## Held-out results

| Outcome | P1.5.1 | Shadow |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Correct gated recommendations, out of six labeled cases | 1 | 1 |
| False-positive gated recommendations | 1 | 1 |
| Gated abstentions on labeled cases | 4 | 4 |
| Gated abstentions on three ambiguous cases | 3 | 3 |
| Correct unique top ranks, out of six labeled cases | 3 | 5 |
| Incorrect unique top ranks on labeled cases | 2 | 1 |
| Tied top rank on labeled cases | 1 | 0 |
| Unique top ranks on three ambiguous cases | 2 | 2 |

The replay contains three simulated corrections, including one held-out live recording with a misleading studio identifier that both models still recommend incorrectly. Four deferral events across the corpus are excluded from choice training. The learner uses three eligible training examples. The existing pairwise sign model gives one point to checked edition slots, removes one point from matching identifiers, and gives one point to the contradictory different-identifier factor. The last adjustment softens that factor’s penalty. These signals reflect the engineered examples and provide no evidence for how a listener reasoned. The recommendation results do not justify changing the production ranker or acceptance policy.

## Order and reproducibility

In the four eligible labeled multi-candidate held-out cases, a simulated always-first reviewer switched identities in all four when the order was reversed. Each order yielded two correct picks. A fixed-identity control switched zero. Human responses numbered zero, so the human order effect remains unknown.

The runner’s `--order-packets` option emits one blinded case per eligible held-out family and rotates identical evidence through every position. An evaluation can randomly assign each reviewer one order per family, balancing assignments across reviewers. Compare candidate selection rates by position within the same case and candidate, and report abstentions and uncertainty alongside any estimated position effect. Stable choices across positions are evidence of preference conditional on the presentation. Score and badge effects would require separate randomization.

Run in a disposable environment:

```sh
RAILS_ENV=test bin/rails runner script/resolution_real_corpus.rb
RAILS_ENV=test bin/rails runner script/resolution_real_corpus.rb --order-packets
```

The benchmark uses `public-catalog-replay-v1`, `recording-ranking-v1`, and `pairwise-factor-sign-v1`. It is offline and has no connection to the P1.5.1a review UI. The next increment is a larger, independently adjudicated corpus with realistic discovery misses and a counterbalanced review study.
